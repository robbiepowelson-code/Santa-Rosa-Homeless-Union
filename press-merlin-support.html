Santa Rosa Homeless Union Rallies to Defend Local Homeowner Threatened with Arrest for Helping Unhoused Neighbors

SANTA ROSA, Calif. —

This Wednesday, the Santa Rosa Homeless Union will stand in support of Merlin, a local homeowner facing threats of arrest and code enforcement action from the City of Santa Rosa. His alleged offense: offering food, shelter, and comfort to unhoused people who have been turned away from the Sam Jones Hall homeless shelter.

The conflict has escalated steadily in recent weeks. According to the Union, Sgt. Barrett of the Santa Rosa Police Department has repeatedly threatened Merlin with arrest. Sgt. Barrett leads a weekly sweep of Finley Avenue — the street where Merlin's family home sits — during which officers seize and destroy the belongings of unhoused people sleeping near the shelter. Many of those affected are disabled seniors who have been removed from Sam Jones Hall and are too disabled to move quickly. Two weeks ago, the Union reports, a disabled woman was arrested while she slept as the sweep arrived.

Merlin, who is sympathetic to his unhoused neighbors, has responded by opening his door during the Wednesday sweeps. He has allowed unhoused people to temporarily move their belongings into his front yard and to take shelter from the police. That act of compassion, the Union says, has drawn retaliation from Sgt. Barrett and his crew, who have threatened Merlin with arrest.

Last week, according to the Union, Sgt. Barrett arrived with plainclothes men who photographed Merlin's home. Sgt. Barrett identified the men as building inspectors and warned Merlin: "If you let people move stuff on your property again, I'll have you arrested."

The Santa Rosa Homeless Union also points to the root of the crisis on Finley Avenue: the operation of the Sam Jones Hall shelter, run by Catholic Charities. Unhoused people staying there, the Union says, are repeatedly removed with little or no notice — creating a constant churn of people pushed out onto Finley Avenue with all of their belongings and nowhere to go.

"If Sam Jones did a better job at managing their program, so that people didn't get kicked out so often or could have time to make alternative plans, then this problem wouldn't be happening," a Union representative said. "It's because Sam Jones is so poorly operated that people are constantly being kicked out onto the street."

The Santa Rosa Homeless Union calls this a troubling escalation — the use of police pressure and the threat of code enforcement to punish a resident for helping his most vulnerable neighbors. The Union is calling on the City of Santa Rosa to halt the threats against Merlin, end the destruction of unhoused residents' property during sweeps, and hold Catholic Charities accountable for the management of Sam Jones Hall so that people are no longer discharged onto the street without notice or a place to go.

The Santa Rosa Homeless Union will gather in support of Merlin this Wednesday. Members, supporters, and press are encouraged to attend.

The Union urges members of the media to attend next Wednesday's sweep in person to witness the seizures and the confrontations firsthand. The sweep runs from 6:30 a.m. until noon on Finley Avenue. Reporters and photographers are welcome for any portion of that window.
